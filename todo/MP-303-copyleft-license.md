## Why

The paradox that kills the paranoia: once the content is explicitly free to
copy (CC BY-SA — share-alike, attribution), "stealing" it becomes meaningless.
All value concentrates in what cannot be forked: the 20-year domain, the name,
the voice, and Pete-being-the-guy. The egg becomes public and *therefore* safe.
It's also literally the family name — copyleftout. The license makes the pun
load-bearing.

## What

1. **Pete decides** — this one is genuinely his call, not a default:
   - CC BY-SA 4.0 for site content (recommended: share-alike means derivatives
     stay free; attribution means Pete's name travels with every copy)
   - License for the code (MIT or GPL — decide with the same logic)
   - What's excluded: licensed images (supergrams_licensed.png suggests paid
     art), third-party media, logos/name. **The moderate Party name and marks
     are not licensed** — that's the egg.
2. LICENSE file + a human-readable licensing note in the site's voice
   ("copy it, it's called copyleftout for a reason — just say where you got it
   and keep it free").
3. Audit img/ and media for third-party rights before declaring anything free.
